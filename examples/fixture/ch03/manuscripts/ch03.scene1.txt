The Association posted the reinspection notice for Mapo Gate 3 at 9:20 a.m., and by 9:40 Do-yoon knew he had broken something.

He read it twice on the hall's public board. *Gate MP-003 — grade under review. Entry suspended pending survey.* Eleven cores, nine of them two grades above envelope, and a clerk who had done her job properly at 7:40 the previous morning.

The first time, nobody had flagged Mapo Gate 3. The first time, there had been no anomalous cores, because the eleventh core had been pulled out of a chamber nobody entered — the chamber that had come down on Park Mu-jin's leg and ended his walking, and with it the run, and with it the count.

Do-yoon had kept an old man's leg. In exchange, the Association was now looking at a gate that should have gone unwatched for another five months.

*It isn't the same river.*

He had told himself for eleven days that his memory was a map. Standing in front of the board he understood that it was a map of a country he had already begun to demolish.

“You're the porter.”

The woman had come up on his left without any of the noise a person normally makes. Association jacket, no rank pin, a tablet held the way people hold a thing they have already finished reading.

“I'm a porter,” Do-yoon said.

“You're the porter who told Park Mu-jin to take the right corridor.” She did not look up from the tablet. “Yoon Ha-eun. Survey division. I have his statement, the team lead's statement, and eleven cores that don't belong in a D-gate.” Now she looked up. “I don't have yours.”

“He asked me for a reason. I gave him a reason.”

“You gave him a third chamber.” She let that sit. “The survey filed nine days before your run counts two. I walked it myself this morning. There are three.”

Do-yoon kept his face still, which was itself a kind of answer, and he knew it.

“Then the survey was wrong,” he said.

“The survey was wrong,” Ha-eun agreed. “That's the part that interests my division. Surveys are wrong about corridors. They are not wrong about rooms.” She tapped the tablet once against her palm. “An F-rank porter walked into a gate he had never entered and corrected a filed survey. Either you have a source inside my division, Mister Kang, or you have something my division doesn't have a form for.”

“I have a bad feeling about ceilings.”

“Mm.” It was not a laugh. “Mu-jin said you'd say something like that.”
